01

What shipped this week

The first working version of the citizen reporting loop: a prototype you can install on a phone, submit a report through, and see listed with its status.

  • ✓ Installable PWA shell Built with Vite + React, configured with a manifest and service worker so it can be added to a phone's home screen like a native app.
  • ✓ Supabase backend Hosted database, file storage, and API — a reports table plus a public bucket for photos, with access rules configured.
  • ✓ Report submission form Category, description, photo capture, and location — via device GPS or by dropping a pin on a map when GPS isn't available or permitted.
  • ✓ Report status list Submitted reports appear with photo, category, and status ("Reported") immediately after submitting.

03

Development plan

Five phases, building outward from the core loop toward the full platform described in the pitch deck.

  1. 1
    Core reporting loop Complete

    Submit a report with photo, category and location; see it listed with a status. This week's work.

  2. 2
    Feed view & lightweight identity Next up

    Turn the status list into a feed, styled like a social timeline: each report shown alongside its resolution once one exists — a before/after record rather than a flat status word. Alongside this, a lightweight way to show "my reports" without full login, and queuing so a report made with no signal sends once one returns.

  3. 3
    AI categorisation & priority Later

    Replace the fixed category dropdown with automatic classification and a priority score, as outlined in the pitch deck.

  4. 4
    Authority routing & verification Later

    Route reports to the responsible local authority, and use AI to verify a fix actually happened before a report is marked resolved.

  5. 5
    Public dashboards & SaaS layer Later

    National dashboards benchmarking authority performance, plus API access for councils, housing associations and waste operators.

04

Risks & open questions

Nothing here blocks continuing — these are the trade-offs made to move fast this week, worth deciding on deliberately before the prototype becomes a pilot.

  • High · Privacy Reports are fully public, with no login

    There's no authentication yet, so every visitor sees every submitted report, not just their own — and anyone with the app's URL can also submit one. Fine for testing; needs a real access model (login, or at minimum a per-device identity) before wider use.

  • High · Offline The app doesn't work without a connection

    Being installable doesn't mean it works offline — the service worker only caches the app itself, not the ability to submit or view reports. Reporting from a dead zone (common for fly-tipping in rural areas) needs a local queue that syncs once signal returns — a distinct piece of work for phase 2.

  • Medium · Compliance Data residency hasn't been decided

    The Supabase project was created in whichever region was convenient for setup, not necessarily the UK/EU. Supabase projects can't change region later without rebuilding from scratch, so this needs a deliberate decision before any real citizen data — location, photos — is collected.

  • Medium · Infrastructure Running on Supabase's free tier

    Fine for a prototype, but the free tier pauses a project after a week of inactivity and caps storage and bandwidth. A pilot with real users will need a paid tier.

  • Medium · Content No moderation on submitted photos

    Once reports are visible to others — and more so once phase 2's feed makes them the main view — inappropriate, duplicate or irrelevant photo submissions become a real question, alongside storage cost as volume grows.

  • Low · Accuracy Manual pin placement is less precise than GPS

    Citizens who decline location permission fall back to tapping a map by hand. Workable now; worth watching once routing needs to match a report to the correct authority boundary.

  • Low · Expected gap Status never changes from "Reported"

    By design for this phase — there's no authority on the other end yet to update it. Flagging it here so it reads as scoped, not broken.

05

Questions for you

We've built as far as we reasonably can from the pitch deck alone. These are the things only you can answer, and they shape how phases 2–4 actually get designed.

  • ? How should a report actually reach the people who act on it? A shared inbox or dashboard your team checks manually, or does it need to plug into a specific system a council already uses?
  • ? Who handles environmental reports today, before Resolve UK? Is there an existing process or contact per council we should route through or mirror, or is this genuinely a gap with nothing in place yet?
  • ? What counts as "resolved," and who decides that? Does the authority self-report completion, is there a review step, or is that exactly the gap AI verification (phase 4) is meant to fill?
  • ? Is there a specific council or region to design the first real integration around? A named pilot partner would let us design routing for one real, concrete case instead of guessing at something generic for all 300+ UK authorities.
  • ? Any existing commitments on data handling we should build to? Promises already made to councils or citizens about privacy, retention, or data residency that should shape the backend decisions now rather than later.
